## User Query
I am working with human somatic whole exome sequencing data from a series of tumor biopsies and have identified several structural variants and copy number alterations that I need to interpret. Since I am still learning the ropes of setting up my annotation pipeline, I was wondering how I should approach this if my primary goal is just to get a quick top-line call on which protein-coding genes are being disrupted or lost by these large genomic changes, without necessarily needing an exhaustive breakdown of every small-scale nucleotide change at this stage.

## Recommendation
### 1. Detected Scenario
- **species**: human (stated: "human somatic whole exome sequencing")
- **origin**: somatic (stated: "series of tumor biopsies")
- **variant_size_class**: structural-CNV (stated: "structural variants and copy number alterations")
- **region_focus**: coding (stated: "protein-coding genes being disrupted")
- **analysis_goal**: basic-consequence (stated: "quick top-line call... without necessarily needing an exhaustive breakdown")

### 2. Recommended Options
✓ core_type [source: core_type, priority=recommended] confidence: high
  Reason: Using the default Ensembl/GENCODE core is the standard approach for human gene annotation.

✓ symbol [source: symbol, priority=recommended] confidence: high
  Reason: Essential for a "top-line call" to provide human-readable HGNC gene symbols (e.g., TP53) rather than just Ensembl IDs.

✓ biotype [source: biotype, priority=recommended] confidence: high
  Reason: Helps confirm that the disrupted regions are indeed protein-coding genes as requested.

✓ tsl [source: tsl, priority=recommended] confidence: high
  Reason: Provides Transcript Support Level to help assess the reliability of the transcript being disrupted.

✓ appris [source: appris, priority=recommended] confidence: high
  Reason: Helps identify functionally important isoforms, which is critical when assessing gene disruption in cancer.

✓ dosage_sensitivity [source: dosage_sensitivity, priority=recommended] confidence: high
  Reason: Highly relevant for CNVs; annotates genes with scores (like pHaplo) indicating how sensitive they are to copy number changes.

✓ numbers [source: numbers, priority=recommended] confidence: high
  Reason: Provides exon/intron numbering to help quickly locate where in the gene the structural change occurs.

✓ loeuf [source: loeuf, priority=recommended] confidence: high
  Reason: Provides gnomAD LOEUF scores to help distinguish between genes that are tolerant vs. intolerant to loss-of-function.

✓ gnomad_sv [source: gnomad_sv, priority=recommended] confidence: high
  Reason: Specifically recommended for structural variants to provide allele frequencies from the gnomAD SV database.

✗ hgvs [source: hgvs] confidence: high
  Reason: Explicitly excluded because the user wants a "top-line call" and to avoid "exhaustive breakdown of every small-scale nucleotide change."

✗ most_severe [source: most_severe] confidence: medium
  Reason: While it simplifies output, it can conflict with the highly useful annotations like `--symbol` and `--tsl` which are needed for the requested "top-line" context.

✗ dbnsfp [source: dbnsfp] confidence: high
  Reason: This is a meta-resource for missense/small-scale variants; not appropriate for structural/CNV analysis.

✗ spliceai [source: spliceai] confidence: high
  Reason: Predicts splicing effects of small SNPs; not applicable to large-scale structural variants/CNVs.

### 3. Generated VEP Command
```bash
vep --input_file <input.vcf> --output_file <output.txt> --cache --symbol --biotype --tsl --appris --dosage_sensitivity --numbers --plugin LOEUF --custom <path_to_gnomad_sv.vcf>,type=exact,overlap_cutoff=exact
```
*Note: On the Ensembl Web Interface, you would select "Gene Symbol", "Transcript Support Level", "Biotype", and "Dosage Sensitivity" under the respective plugin/output sections.*

## User Query
I’m working through some human tumour sequencing data where we're specifically looking for large-scale changes in the DNA structure, like deletions or duplications. A lot of these structural changes are landing in the regions outside of the actual genes, so I need a way to quickly figure out if these stretches of missing or extra DNA might be affecting how genes are turned on or off. It would also be really helpful to see how common these types of variations are in the general healthy population so I can better judge whether a finding looks like a potential driver for the cancer or just a common variation.

  Reason: Necessary to identify if these structural variants overlap with known variants in databases like ClinVar or gnomAD.

  Reason: Crucial for clinical interpretation to see if the identified structural changes have been previously reported as pathogenic or benign.

  Reason: Standard requirement to define the transcript/gene annotation set driving the consequence calculation.
✓ dosage_sensitivity [source: dosage_sensitivity, priority=recommended] confidence: high
  Reason: Highly relevant for CNVs/structural variants to assess the impact of gene dosage changes (e.g., haploinsufficiency).
✓ gnomad_sv [source: gnomad_sv, priority=recommended] confidence: high
  Reason: Specifically required to provide population frequency context for structural variants using gnomAD SV data.
✓ phenotypes [source: phenotypes, priority=recommended] confidence: high
  Reason: Helps in clinical interpretation by linking overlapping genomic features to known disease phenotypes.
✓ regulatory [source: regulatory, priority=recommended] confidence: high
  Reason: Directly addresses the goal of identifying if structural changes affect promoters, enhancers, or other regulatory elements.
✓ symbol [source: symbol, priority=recommended] confidence: high
  Reason: Provides HGNC gene symbols, making the clinical interpretation of disrupted genes much more intuitive.
✗ cadd [source: cadd] confidence: high
  Reason: CADD is a predictor for small-scale (SNV/Indel) variants and is not applicable to structural-CNV analysis.
✗ hgvs [source: hgvs] confidence: high
  Reason: HGVS nomenclature is primarily used for small-scale nucleotide/amino acid changes and is not applicable for CNVs in this context.
✗ loeuf [source: loeuf] confidence: high
  Reason: LOEUF scores are designed for loss-of-function small variants, not structural variations.
✗ most_severe [source: most_severe] confidence: high
  Reason: Enabling this would conflict with the ability to output multiple regulatory/biotype annotations for a single structural event.
✗ protein [source: protein] confidence: high
  Reason: The focus is non-coding/regulatory; protein identifiers are not applicable to the primary region of interest.
✗ sift [source: sift] confidence: high
  Reason: SIFT is a missense variant predictor and is not applicable to structural variants or regulatory regions.
✗ spliceai [source: spliceai] confidence: high
  Reason: SpliceAI predicts the effect of small variants on splicing; it is not designed for CNV analysis.

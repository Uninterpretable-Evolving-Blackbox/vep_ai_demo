## User Query
what does a missense_variant mean, briefly?

## Explanation
A **[missense_variant]** is a type of mutation where a single nucleotide change in the DNA results in the replacement of one amino acid with a different one in the protein sequence.

### What it means
In plain language, the "recipe" for the protein has been altered at one specific spot. The protein is still the same length and the rest of the sequence remains correct, but one "link" in the protein chain has been swapped for another.

### Why VEP assigned this consequence
VEP assigns this term because a substitution occurred in a coding region (an exon) that changed the codon (the triplet of nucleotides) to specify a different amino acid. Because the reading frame is not disrupted (unlike a `frameshift_variant`), the impact is categorized as **MODERATE**. The severity depends on:
*   **Biochemical change:** Does the new amino acid have similar properties to the old one, or is it chemically very different (e.g., changing a small neutral amino acid to a large charged one)?
*   **Location:** Is this change happening in a part of the protein that is critical for its function, such as a binding site or the structural core?

### What to check next
Since a missense variant is not automatically "broken," you need to determine if this specific swap is harmful or benign. You should:
1.  **Use Pathogenicity Predictors:** Check tools like **SIFT, PolyPhen-2, CADD, AlphaMissense, or REVEL** to see if algorithms predict the change will be damaging.
2.  **Check Conservation:** Look at whether that specific amino acid position is "conserved" (identical) across different species, which often implies that mutations there are biologically significant.
3.  **Consult Population Data:** Check databases like **gnomAD** to see if this variant is common in the general population (suggesting it is benign) or extremely rare (suggesting it could be pathogenic).

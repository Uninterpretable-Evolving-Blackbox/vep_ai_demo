## User Query
I am looking at some human tumor sequencing results and I have noticed several large sections of DNA that seem to be either extra or missing. I am especially interested in the areas that don't actually make proteins but instead act as control switches for genes, as I want to see if these big changes might be affecting how those genes work and contributing to the cancer. It is also really important for me to know how common these same types of variations are in healthy people so I can tell the difference between normal variation and something potentially dangerous. How should I configure my run to get all that info about the regulatory impact and the population background?

  Reason: Essential to identify the type of regulatory feature or non-coding element being disrupted.
✓ check_existing [source: check_existing, priority=optional] confidence: high
  Reason: Required as a dependency to retrieve ClinVar and population frequency data.
✓ clinvar [source: clinvar, priority=recommended] confidence: high
  Reason: Crucial for clinical interpretation to see if identified CNVs have known pathogenic associations.
✓ dosage_sensitivity [source: dosage_sensitivity, priority=recommended] confidence: high
  Reason: Highly relevant for CNVs to determine if the gene affected is sensitive to changes in copy number.
✓ gnomad_sv [source: gnomad_sv, priority=recommended] confidence: high
  Reason: Specifically provides population allele frequencies for structural variants to distinguish somatic drivers from germline variation.
✓ regulatory [source: regulatory, priority=recommended] confidence: high
  Reason: Necessary to detect overlaps with promoters, enhancers, and other control switches mentioned in the scenario.
✓ symbol [source: symbol, priority=no priority] confidence: medium
  Reason: Adds HGNC gene symbols, making the output more human-readable for clinical reporting.

✗ af_gnomade [source: af_gnomade] confidence: high
  Reason: This plugin provides exome-based frequencies; for structural variants, gnomAD_SV is the appropriate tool.
✗ cadd [source: cadd] confidence: high
  Reason: CADD is primarily optimized for single nucleotide variants and small indels, not large-scale CNVs.
✗ hgvs [source: hgvs] confidence: high
  Reason: HGVS nomenclature is not applicable to large structural/CNV descriptions in this context.
✗ most_severe [source: most_severe] confidence: high
  Reason: Disabling to allow full annotation of regulatory and population data without restricting to a single consequence.
✗ protein [source: protein] confidence: high
  Reason: The user is explicitly focusing on non-coding/regulatory regions, making protein identifiers less relevant.

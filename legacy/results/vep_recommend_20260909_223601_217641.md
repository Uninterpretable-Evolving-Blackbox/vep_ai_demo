## User Query
I am processing human germline sequencing data from a large-scale population cohort and have identified several large structural changes, such as deletions and duplications of DNA segments. I want to focus my analysis on these changes when they occur in regulatory regions that control gene activity, rather than just looking at the protein-coding parts of the genome. For these types of variations, I need to ensure my annotation process includes information on how common these changes are within the general population so I can identify which ones are truly rare.

  Reason: Necessary to drive consequence calculation using the Ensembl/GENCODE core annotation set.
✓ symbol [source: symbol, priority=NOT APPLICABLE] confidence: high
  Reason: Essential for mapping structural variants to their nearest/overlapping gene names for biological context.
✓ biotype [source: biotype, priority=recommended] confidence: high
  Reason: Helps distinguish if the structural variant affects a protein-coding gene, a lincRNA, or other regulatory elements.
✓ regulatory [source: regulatory, priority=recommended] confidence: high
  Reason: Directly addresses the user's requirement to identify overlaps with regulatory regions (promoters, enhancers, etc.).
✓ gnomad_sv [source: gnomad_sv, priority=recommended] confidence: high
  Reason: Specifically provides allele frequencies for structural variants from the gnomAD SV database to identify rarity.
✓ check_existing [source: check_existing, priority=optional] confidence: medium
  Reason: Useful for identifying if the structural change is already catalogued in databases like dbSNP.
✗ hgvs [source: hgvs, priority=NOT APPLICABLE] confidence: high
  Reason: HGVS nomenclature is primarily used for short variants/coding changes; less applicable/standard for large CNVs in this context.
✗ dbnsfp [source: dbnsfp, priority=NOT APPLICABLE] confidence: high
  Reason: dbNSFP is a meta-resource for missense (single nucleotide) variants and is not applicable to structural variants.
✗ sift [source: sift, priority=NOT APPLICABLE] confidence: high
  Reason: SIFT predicts the impact of amino acid substitutions; it is not applicable to structural variations or non-coding regions.
✗ polyphen [source: polyphen, priority=NOT APPLICABLE] confidence: high
  Reason: PolyPhen predicts protein structure impact; it is not applicable to structural variants or non-coding regions.
✗ cadd [source: cadd, priority=no priority] confidence: high
  Reason: CADD scores are optimized for small variants (SNPs/indels) rather than large-scale CNVs.
✗ most_severe [source: most_severe, priority=no priority] confidence: medium
  Reason: For structural variants, you want to see all overlapping features (genes, enhancers) rather than a single collapsed consequence.
✗ summary [source: summary, priority=no priority] confidence: medium
  Reason: The user needs detailed regulatory and population info, not a collapsed summary.

what does splice_donor_variant mean?

## Explanation
The term **[splice_donor_variant]** refers to a mutation that occurs at the beginning of an intron (the non-coding region between exons).

### What it means
This variant is classified as having a **HIGH** impact. It occurs at the "donor" site—the specific location where the cellular machinery (the spliceosome) is supposed to "cut" the RNA to remove the intron. 

### The Biological Mechanism
To correctly assemble a protein, the cell must precisely remove introns and join exons together. This process relies on highly conserved chemical signals. The most critical signal at the start of an intron is the **GT dinucleotide**. 

When a variant disrupts this GT signal, the spliceosome may no longer recognize the boundary between the exon and the intron. This failure typically leads to one of three errors:
1.  **Exon Skipping:** The cell misses the signal and accidentally removes a piece of the coding sequence (an exon) along with the intron.
2.  **Intron Retention:** The cell fails to remove the intron entirely, leaving non-coding "junk" DNA in the final mRNA.
3.  **Cryptic Splice Site Activation:** The cell searches for the next best "GT" signal nearby, which may result in a protein with an incorrect sequence or length.

Any of these errors usually result in a **frameshift** or a **premature stop codon**, often leading to a non-functional protein or the degradation of the mRNA via nonsense-mediated decay (NMD).

### What to check next
Because this variant affects the structure of the mRNA, you should look into the following:
*   **Splicing Predictors:** Use computational tools like **SpliceAI** or **MaxEntScan** to predict how much the mutation actually weakens the splice site.
*   **Population Frequency:** Check databases like **gnomAD** to see if this variant is common in the general population (which might suggest it is a benign polymorphism) or very rare (which increases the suspicion of pathogenicity).
*   **Clinical Databases:** Check **ClinVar** to see if this specific splice-site mutation has already been documented in patients with known genetic diseases.

## User Query
I am starting to set up the pipeline for our human tumor-only whole genome sequencing data from a large cancer cohort, but I am still a bit unsure about how to best capture all the relevant information. We have identified several structural variants and copy number alterations, and my goal is to get a quick top-line summary of their functional consequences. Specifically, I need to see how these changes affect protein-coding regions—ideally identifying impacts on the most reliable transcripts—but I also want to capture any potential disruptions to regulatory or non-coding elements. Additionally, it would be really helpful if the results provided context regarding population allele frequencies so I can distinguish these somatic hits from common germline variation.

### 2. Recommended Options
✓ core_type [source: core_type, priority=recommended] confidence: high
  Reason: Essential for driving the consequence calculation using the Ensembl/GENCODE primary annotation set.
✓ symbol [source: symbol, priority=recommended] confidence: high
  Reason: Provides gene symbols (e.g., BRCA1) to make the functional consequences human-readable.
✓ biotype [source: biotype, priority=recommended] confidence: high
  Reason: Necessary to distinguish between protein-coding, lincRNA, and other regulatory biotypes.
✓ regulatory [source: regulatory, priority=recommended] confidence: high
  Reason: Required to capture the requested disruptions to regulatory and non-coding elements.
✓ gnomad_sv [source: gnomad_sv, priority=recommended] confidence: high
  Reason: Specifically designed to annotate structural variants with gnomAD SV allele frequencies to filter common germline variation.
✓ tsl [source: tsl, priority=recommended] confidence: high
  Reason: Helps identify the "most reliable transcripts" by providing Transcript Support Level rankings.
✓ appris [source: appris, priority=recommended] confidence: high
  Reason: Assists in identifying functionally important isoforms, aiding the goal of finding impacts on reliable transcripts.
✓ dosage_sensitivity [source: dosage_sensitivity, priority=recommended] confidence: high
  Reason: Highly relevant for CNVs to identify if the affected gene is sensitive to dosage changes (e.g., haploinsufficiency).
✓ check_existing [source: check_existing, priority=optional] confidence: medium
  Reason: Useful to check if the SVs are already cataloged in databases.
✓ clinvar [source: clinvar, priority=optional] confidence: medium
  Reason: Optional but helpful for assessing the clinical relevance of the identified structural changes.
✗ hgvs [source: hgvs] confidence: high
  Reason: HGVS nomenclature is primarily used for small variants (SNVs/indels) and is not applicable/useful for CNVs/SVs in this context.
✗ sift [source: sift] confidence: high
  Reason: SIFT is a missense predictor for small variants and is NOT APPLICABLE to structural variants.
✗ polyphen [source: polyphen] confidence: high
  Reason: PolyPhen is a missense predictor for small variants and is NOT APPLICABLE to structural variants.
✗ cadd [source: cadd] confidence: high
  Reason: CADD is optimized for small variants and is NOT APPLICABLE to structural variants.
✗ most_severe [source: most_severe] confidence: high
  Reason: The user requested a "top-line summary" but also specific regulatory/non-coding info; disabling `most_severe` ensures they see the full range of consequences (coding + regulatory) rather than just one collapsed term.
✗ summary [source: summary] confidence: high
  Reason: While a "summary" was requested, the `--summary` flag collapses all data into a single line, which would hide the regulatory and biotype details requested.

### 3. Generated VEP Command
```

```

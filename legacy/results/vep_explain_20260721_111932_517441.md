## User Query
Why is my variant annotated as splice_donor_variant when it is 3bp into the intron?

## Explanation
Based on the VEP definitions provided, there is a discrepancy between your observation (a variant 3bp into the intron) and the annotation `splice_donor_variant`.

According to the VEP consequence terms:

*   **[splice_donor_variant] (impact: HIGH):** This term is specifically reserved for variants that disrupt the essential **GT dinucleotide** at the 5' splice site (positions +1 and +2 of the intron). This disruption prevents the spliceosome from recognizing the boundary, often leading to severe consequences like exon skipping or intron retention.
*   **[splice_donor_region_variant] (impact: LOW):** This term is used for variants that fall in the donor splice region, specifically positions **+3 to +6** of the intron. These positions contribute to splice site strength but do not involve the critical GT dinucleotide.
*   **[splice_region_variant] (impact: LOW):** This term applies to variants near a splice site, defined as being up to 8 bases into the intron (e.g., **3–8 bases into the intron**).

### Why you might be seeing this annotation
If your variant is truly at position +3 of the intron, VEP should technically annotate it as `splice_donor_region_variant` or `splice_region_variant`, not `splice_donor_variant`. 

If you are seeing `splice_donor_variant`, one of the following may be occurring:
1.  **Coordinate Discrepancy:** The variant might actually be at position +1 or +2 (the GT dinucleotide), even if it appears to be at +3 in your initial assessment. 
2.  **Transcript Annotation overlap:** The variant might overlap a different transcript where the junction is positioned differently, causing VEP to flag the canonical GT site as disrupted.

### What you should check next
If a variant located at the edge of a splice site (like position +3) is suspected of being pathogenic, do not rely solely on the VEP consequence term or the "LOW" impact prediction. You should investigate the functional impact on splicing:

1.  **Verify Coordinates:** Double-check your variant's position relative to the transcript's exon-intron junction (specifically looking at positions +1 and +2).
2.  **Use Splicing Predictors:** For variants in the `splice_donor_region_variant` or `splice_region_variant` categories, use tools like **SpliceAI** or **MaxEntScan**. These tools can determine if a mutation 3bp away from the junction is actually strong enough to weaken the splice site and cause splicing errors.
3.  **Check Splice Site Strength:** Even "LOW" impact variants near the junction can sometimes create "cryptic" splice sites, which VEP flags as high-impact biological consequences in some contexts, even if the term used is lower-impact.

## User Query
My VEP output has splice_acceptor_variant with IMPACT=HIGH and a MANE_SELECT flag. What does that actually mean for my patient's variant?

## Explanation
This VEP output indicates a highly significant finding. When you see a `splice_acceptor_variant` with a **HIGH** impact paired with the `MANE_SELECT` flag, it means the variant is located in a critical part of the most clinically relevant version of that gene.

Here is the breakdown of what this means for your patient:

### 1. The Biological Impact: [term: splice_acceptor_variant]
The term `splice_acceptor_variant` (impact: **HIGH**) means the mutation occurs at the junction where an intron (a non-coding section of DNA) is supposed to end and an exon (a coding section) is supposed to begin.

*   **The Mechanism:** Splicing relies on a highly conserved **AG** dinucleotide at the 3' end of the intron. This variant disrupts that "AG" signal. Because the spliceosome (the cellular machinery) can no longer recognize where the intron ends, it may skip the next exon entirely or fail to remove the intron (intron retention).
*   **The Consequence:** This typically leads to a massive change in the protein's reading frame or the introduction of a premature stop codon. In most cases, this results in a non-functional protein or the degradation of the mRNA via nonsense-mediated decay (NMD).

### 2. The Clinical Significance: [MANE_SELECT]
The `MANE_SELECT` flag is a vital piece of "meta-information." It stands for **Matched Annotation from NCBI and Ensembl**.

*   **What it means:** There are often many different "isoforms" (versions) of a single gene. The `MANE_SELECT` flag indicates that the specific transcript being annotated is the "gold standard" transcript used by both NCBI (RefSeq) and Ensembl.
*   **Why it matters for your patient:** This is the transcript most frequently used in clinical research and diagnostic reporting. Because the variant is on the `MANE_SELECT` transcript, you can be highly confident that this is the primary, functional version of the gene that matters for the patient's phenotype. It reduces the ambiguity often found when a variant falls on a "noisy" or less-important transcript.

### Summary for Clinical Interpretation
In plain language: **You have found a mutation in the "master" version of this gene that is likely to break the instructions for making the protein by preventing the cell from correctly cutting and pasting the genetic code.**

### Suggested Next Steps
Since this is a splice site variant, the primary goal is to confirm if the "broken" AG signal actually causes a splicing error in practice.

1.  **Check Splicing Predictors:** Use tools like **SpliceAI** or **MaxEntScan**. These computational tools can predict whether this specific change actually strengthens or weakens the splice site and if it might create a "cryptic" (fake) splice site.
2.  **Check Population Frequency:** Look up the variant in **gnomAD**. If this specific `splice_acceptor_variant` is found frequently in healthy populations, it is less likely to be the cause of a rare disease.
3.  **Check Clinical Databases:** Search **ClinVar** to see if this specific transcript/coordinate has been previously reported as "Pathogenic" or "Likely Pathogenic."
4.  **Look for Functional Evidence:** Search PubMed for existing studies on this gene that use RNA-seq to demonstrate how acceptor mutations affect protein expression.
